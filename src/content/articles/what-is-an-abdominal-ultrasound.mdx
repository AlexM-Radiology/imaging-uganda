An abdominal ultrasound is a safe, non-invasive medical imaging examination that uses high-frequency sound waves to produce pictures of the organs and structures inside your abdomen (belly). It helps healthcare professionals investigate abdominal pain, swelling, abnormal blood tests and other medical conditions.

Unlike X-rays and CT scans, an ultrasound does not use ionizing radiation. It is commonly used to examine organs such as the liver, gallbladder, kidneys, pancreas, spleen and abdominal aorta.

## How Does an Abdominal Ultrasound Work?
An abdominal ultrasound uses a small handheld device called a transducer to send sound waves into the body. These waves bounce off internal organs and tissues, producing echoes that are converted into real-time images on a monitor.

During the examination, the sonographer or appropriately trained healthcare professional applies ultrasound gel to your abdomen and moves the transducer across the skin to obtain images from different angles.

The procedure is generally painless, although you may experience mild discomfort if the transducer presses against a tender area.

## What Organs Does an Abdominal Ultrasound Examine?

A routine abdominal ultrasound can assess several important organs and structures.

1. **Liver.**
The scan evaluates the liver's size, shape, texture and internal appearance. It can help identify fatty liver changes, cysts, masses, signs suggestive of cirrhosis and certain features of liver disease.

2. **Gallbladder.**
The gallbladder stores bile produced by the liver. Ultrasound can detect gallstones, gallbladder wall thickening, distension and surrounding fluid that may suggest inflammation.

3. **Kidneys.**
The scan assesses kidney size, shape and internal structure. It can help identify kidney stones when visible, hydronephrosis (dilatation of the urine-collecting system), cysts and some kidney masses.

4. **Pancreas.**
The pancreas may be assessed for changes in size, appearance, masses and dilatation of the pancreatic duct. However, it may be partly or completely obscured by bowel gas.

5. **Spleen.**
Ultrasound can assess spleen size and identify certain focal lesions, cysts and other abnormalities.

6. **Abdominal aorta.**
The abdominal aorta is the major blood vessel carrying blood from the heart to the lower body. Ultrasound can detect enlargement suggestive of an abdominal aortic aneurysm.

7. **Urinary bladder.**
Depending on the examination requested, the bladder may also be assessed for its degree of filling, wall appearance, stones and other abnormalities.

## Why Would a Doctor Request an Abdominal Ultrasound?
Your healthcare provider may recommend an abdominal ultrasound for several reasons, including:
- Persistent or unexplained abdominal pain.
- Abdominal swelling or a palpable lump.
- Suspected gallstones or gallbladder inflammation.
- Suspected kidney stones or urinary obstruction.
- Abnormal liver function tests.
- Suspected fatty liver or other liver disease.
- Yellowing of the eyes or skin (jaundice).
- Follow-up of previously identified cysts or masses.
- Assessment of fluid within the abdominal cavity (ascites).
- Evaluation of certain abdominal injuries or other suspected abnormalities.

## What Can an Abdominal Ultrasound Detect?
An abdominal ultrasound may identify a range of conditions, including:
- Gallstones: Stones within the gallbladder.
- Fatty liver: Increased liver echogenicity that may suggest fat accumulation.
- Hydronephrosis: Dilatation of the kidney's collecting system, which can occur when urine flow is obstructed.
- Liver cysts: Fluid-filled structures within the liver.
- Ascites: Abnormal accumulation of fluid in the abdominal cavity.
- Splenomegaly: Enlargement of the spleen.
- Abdominal masses: Abnormal growths that may require further investigation.
- Abdominal aortic aneurysm: Abnormal enlargement of the abdominal aorta.

Ultrasound findings do not always establish a final diagnosis. Depending on the results, your clinician may recommend laboratory tests, CT, MRI or another examination.

## How Do You Prepare for an Abdominal Ultrasound?
Preparation depends on the type of scan and the organs being examined.

For many routine upper abdominal ultrasounds, you may be asked to fast for approximately 6–8 hours before the examination. Fasting helps reduce gallbladder contraction after eating and may decrease interference from bowel gas.

Your imaging facility may also advise you to:
- Follow the fasting instructions provided when booking your appointment.
- Ask whether you should take your usual medicines with a small amount of water.
- Bring your referral form and any previous imaging reports.
- Wear comfortable clothing that allows easy access to your abdomen.

If your examination includes the pelvis or urinary bladder, you may be asked to arrive with a comfortably full bladder. This may require drinking water before the scan.

Do not fast or change your medication without checking the instructions provided by your healthcare professional, particularly if you have diabetes or another condition that makes fasting difficult.

## What Happens During the Examination?
An abdominal ultrasound is usually straightforward and does not require needles or injections.

The procedure typically involves the following steps:
- You will lie on an examination couch, usually on your back.
- The sonographer will apply ultrasound gel to your abdomen.
- A transducer will be moved over your skin to capture images of the organs.
- You may be asked to turn onto your side, take a deep breath or briefly hold your breath to improve the images.
- The examination will be completed, and the gel will be wiped off.

The scan commonly takes around 15–30 minutes, although more complex examinations may take longer.

## Is an Abdominal Ultrasound Safe?
Yes. Diagnostic ultrasound is generally considered safe when performed appropriately by trained professionals.

It uses sound waves rather than ionizing radiation, making it useful for examining many abdominal conditions. It is also commonly used during pregnancy when medically indicated.

As with any imaging test, its usefulness depends on the clinical question, the quality of the images and the experience of the person performing and interpreting the examination.

## What Are the Limitations of an Abdominal Ultrasound?
Although ultrasound is useful, it cannot detect every abdominal condition.

Image quality may be affected by:
- Bowel gas, which can obscure the pancreas and other structures.
- Body habitus, which may make deeper organs more difficult to visualize.
- Small lesions that are difficult to distinguish.
- The location of an abnormality.
- The specific ultrasound equipment and examination technique.

A normal ultrasound does not exclude every possible cause of abdominal pain. If symptoms persist or clinical suspicion remains high, further assessment may be necessary.

## Frequently Asked Questions
<details>
  <summary>Is an abdominal ultrasound painful?</summary>
  <p>No. The procedure is generally painless. Mild pressure may cause temporary discomfort if the area being examined is tender.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>How long does an abdominal ultrasound take?</summary>
  <p>Many routine examinations take approximately 15–30 minutes, depending on the organs assessed and the complexity of the findings.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Does an abdominal ultrasound use radiation?</summary>
  <p>No. Ultrasound uses high-frequency sound waves rather than ionizing radiation.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Can an abdominal ultrasound detect cancer?</summary>
  <p>Ultrasound can help identify masses or abnormalities in the abdomen, but it may not be sufficient to diagnose cancer definitively. Additional tests or biopsies may be needed for a conclusive diagnosis.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Can I eat before an abdominal ultrasound?</summary>
  <p>It is generally recommended to fast for 6-8 hours before an abdominal ultrasound to ensure optimal image quality. However, always follow the specific instructions provided by your healthcare provider.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Can an abdominal ultrasound detect pregnancy?</summary>
  <p>An abdominal ultrasound can sometimes identify a pregnancy, depending on its stage and the examination performed. Early pregnancy assessment often requires a dedicated pelvic ultrasound, sometimes transvaginal ultrasound, and clinical or laboratory correlation.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Is an abdominal ultrasound the same as a pelvic ultrasound?</summary>
  <p>While both are types of ultrasound examinations, they focus on different areas of the body. An abdominal ultrasound examines the organs within the abdomen, while a pelvic ultrasound focuses on the reproductive organs and surrounding structures in the pelvis.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>When Should You Seek Medical Attention?</summary>
  <p>Seek urgent medical assessment if you experience severe or sudden abdominal pain, fainting, persistent vomiting, fever with severe abdominal pain, or yellowing of the eyes accompanied by significant illness.</p>
  <p>An ultrasound may be part of the investigation, but urgent symptoms should not be ignored while waiting for a scan.</p>
</details>

## Conclusion

An abdominal ultrasound is a widely used imaging examination that helps healthcare professionals evaluate the liver, gallbladder, kidneys, pancreas, spleen and other abdominal structures. It is non-invasive, does not use ionizing radiation and can provide valuable information when investigating many abdominal symptoms.

Understanding the procedure and preparing correctly can help you get the most out of your examination. Always discuss your symptoms and ultrasound findings with a qualified healthcare professional.
