# What Does an Ultrasound Scan Show? A Complete Guide

An ultrasound scan is a medical imaging test that uses high-frequency sound waves to produce pictures of structures inside the body. It helps healthcare professionals examine internal organs, investigate symptoms, monitor pregnancy and identify abnormalities without using ionizing radiation.

Ultrasound is widely used in hospitals, clinics and diagnostic imaging centres across Uganda and Africa because it is versatile, non-invasive and provides real-time images.

But what exactly can an ultrasound scan show? The answer depends on the part of the body being examined and the type of ultrasound performed.

## 1. What Can an Ultrasound Scan Show?

An ultrasound scan can examine many organs and tissues, including the abdomen, pelvis, blood vessels, thyroid gland, breasts, muscles, tendons and developing baby during pregnancy.

Common findings include:

- Organ enlargement or changes in organ structure.
- Fluid-filled cysts and some solid masses.
- Gallstones and certain kidney stones.
- Fluid accumulation within the abdomen or around some organs.
- Dilatation of the urinary collecting system, known as hydronephrosis.
- Blood flow abnormalities when Doppler ultrasound is used.
- Pregnancy development and certain pregnancy-related complications.
- Tendon injuries, muscle abnormalities and some joint conditions.

Ultrasound does not detect every disease, and some findings require additional tests to establish a diagnosis.

## 2. What Does an Abdominal Ultrasound Show?

An abdominal ultrasound examines organs and structures within the abdomen.

**Liver:** It can show liver enlargement, increased echogenicity suggestive of fatty liver, cysts, some masses and features that may suggest chronic liver disease.

**Gallbladder:** It can identify gallstones, gallbladder wall thickening, distension and findings that may indicate inflammation.

**Kidneys:** It can assess kidney size and structure, identify hydronephrosis, detect many cysts and show some stones or masses.

**Pancreas:** It can demonstrate certain pancreatic abnormalities, including some masses and duct dilatation. However, bowel gas may limit visualization.

**Spleen:** It can identify splenic enlargement, cysts and some focal lesions.

**Abdominal aorta:** It can detect abnormal enlargement, including an abdominal aortic aneurysm.

**Urinary bladder:** When included in the examination, it can demonstrate bladder distension, some stones, wall abnormalities and residual urine after urination.

The organs assessed depend on the examination requested and the clinical indication.

## 3. What Does a Pelvic Ultrasound Show?

A pelvic ultrasound examines organs in the lower abdomen and pelvis. It may be performed through the lower abdominal wall or, when appropriate, using a transvaginal approach.

In females, pelvic ultrasound can assess:

- The uterus, including its size, shape and lining.
- Uterine fibroids and some other uterine abnormalities.
- The ovaries, including ovarian cysts and masses.
- Features that may suggest polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), when interpreted alongside clinical and laboratory findings.
- Fluid within the pelvis.
- Early pregnancy and certain pregnancy complications.

In males, a pelvic ultrasound may assess the urinary bladder, prostate region and other pelvic structures, depending on the examination.

A transvaginal ultrasound can provide more detailed images of the uterus and ovaries than an abdominal approach in many circumstances.

## 4. What Does an Obstetric Ultrasound Show?

An obstetric ultrasound is performed during pregnancy to assess the developing baby and the pregnancy environment.

Depending on the stage of pregnancy, it can show:

- The location of the pregnancy.
- The presence of an embryo or fetus and cardiac activity when expected and detectable.
- Gestational age and estimated due date.
- Fetal growth and measurements.
- The number of fetuses.
- The position of the placenta.
- The amount of amniotic fluid.
- Certain structural abnormalities.
- Fetal position later in pregnancy.

Some pregnancy complications, including ectopic pregnancy, can be identified through ultrasound assessment. However, very early pregnancy findings may be inconclusive and sometimes require follow-up scans and blood tests.

Ultrasound cannot detect every fetal abnormality, and a normal examination does not guarantee that every condition has been excluded.

## 5. What Does a Thyroid Ultrasound Show?

A thyroid ultrasound examines the thyroid gland in the front of the neck.

It can identify:

- Thyroid nodules.
- Thyroid enlargement, known as goitre.
- Cysts within the thyroid.
- Changes in gland texture.
- Features that may increase or decrease suspicion of malignancy.
- Abnormalities of nearby lymph nodes.

Ultrasound can help determine whether a nodule requires further assessment, but it cannot independently establish whether every nodule is cancerous. Some nodules require fine-needle aspiration or other investigations.

## 6. What Does a Breast Ultrasound Show?

Breast ultrasound is used to evaluate breast lumps, pain and other breast abnormalities.

It can help distinguish fluid-filled cysts from solid masses and assess the appearance of suspicious areas. It may also be used to guide needle sampling or drainage procedures.

Breast ultrasound is particularly useful in evaluating palpable lumps and as a complementary examination to mammography when indicated.

However, it does not replace mammography or other recommended breast investigations in every patient.

## 7. What Does Doppler Ultrasound Show?

Doppler ultrasound assesses the movement of blood through vessels.

It can help evaluate:

- Blood flow in arteries and veins.
- Narrowing or blockage of certain blood vessels.
- Deep vein thrombosis (DVT), a blood clot in a deep vein.
- Blood flow in the carotid arteries of the neck.
- Blood flow in abdominal organs.
- Blood circulation in pregnancy when Doppler assessment is indicated.
- Blood flow within or around some masses.

The usefulness of Doppler ultrasound depends on the vessel being examined, the technique used and the clinical question.

## 8. Can an Ultrasound Scan Detect Cancer?

Yes, ultrasound can identify masses and other abnormalities that may be suspicious for cancer. It can examine organs such as the liver, kidneys, thyroid, breasts and ovaries.

However, **ultrasound alone cannot reliably confirm or exclude all cancers**.

Some benign conditions resemble cancer on imaging, while some cancers have subtle appearances. Depending on the findings, a healthcare professional may recommend additional imaging, laboratory investigations or a biopsy.

## 9. Can an Ultrasound Scan Show Inflammation or Infection?

Ultrasound can demonstrate findings associated with certain infections and inflammatory conditions.

Examples include:

- Gallbladder inflammation.
- Fluid collections or abscesses in accessible areas.
- Some changes associated with appendicitis.
- Kidney swelling or certain complications of urinary infection.
- Pelvic inflammatory changes in selected cases.
- Inflamed superficial soft tissues.

Not every infection is visible on ultrasound. The findings must be interpreted alongside symptoms, physical examination and laboratory results.

## 10. Can an Ultrasound Scan Show Muscle, Tendon and Joint Problems?

Musculoskeletal ultrasound examines accessible muscles, tendons, ligaments and soft tissues.

It may show:

- Tendon tears or tendinopathy.
- Muscle tears and haematomas.
- Joint effusions, meaning excess fluid within a joint.
- Superficial soft-tissue lumps.
- Certain inflammatory changes.
- Some foreign bodies beneath the skin.

Ultrasound can also assess structures dynamically while a patient moves. However, it is less suitable for evaluating some deep structures and many bone abnormalities.

## 11. What Does a Normal Ultrasound Scan Mean?

A normal ultrasound report generally means that the structures examined have no significant abnormality identified within the limitations of the examination.

For example, a normal abdominal ultrasound may describe the liver, gallbladder, spleen and kidneys as having unremarkable appearances, with no significant abnormalities detected.

However, a normal scan does not exclude every possible cause of symptoms. Some conditions are too early, too small or not well visualized by ultrasound.

If symptoms persist, further clinical assessment may still be necessary.

## 12. What Are the Limitations of Ultrasound?

Although ultrasound is a valuable diagnostic tool, it has limitations.

**Bowel gas:** Gas can block sound waves and obscure organs such as the pancreas.

**Body habitus:** Greater tissue depth can make some structures more difficult to visualize.

**Bone and air:** Ultrasound cannot adequately image through bone or air-filled lungs, limiting its use for certain conditions.

**Small or deep abnormalities:** Some lesions may be difficult to detect, depending on their size, location and the equipment used.

**Operator dependence:** Image quality and diagnostic accuracy depend partly on the technique and experience of the examiner.

When ultrasound cannot answer the clinical question, a healthcare professional may recommend CT, MRI, X-ray, laboratory testing or another appropriate investigation.

## 13. Is an Ultrasound Scan Safe?

Diagnostic ultrasound is generally considered safe when used appropriately by trained professionals. It uses sound waves rather than ionizing radiation and is widely used during pregnancy and in other medical examinations.

The examination is usually painless and does not require injections, although some specialized ultrasound procedures may involve additional steps.

The type of scan and any preparation required depend on the body part being examined.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does an ultrasound scan show all organs in the body?

No. Ultrasound can examine many organs and tissues, but it cannot visualize every structure equally well. The examination is selected according to the area of concern.

### Can an ultrasound scan detect kidney stones?

Ultrasound can detect many kidney stones, particularly those in accessible locations, and can identify hydronephrosis associated with urinary obstruction. However, some stones may not be visible, and CT may be required in selected cases.

### Can an ultrasound scan detect pregnancy?

Yes. Ultrasound can identify and assess pregnancy, although findings depend on gestational age and the approach used. Early pregnancy sometimes requires repeat imaging or blood tests.

### Can an ultrasound scan detect abdominal pain?

Ultrasound does not directly measure pain. It can help identify possible causes, such as gallstones, hydronephrosis, pelvic abnormalities or fluid accumulation. A normal scan does not exclude all causes of abdominal pain.

### Can an ultrasound scan detect a blood clot?

Doppler ultrasound is commonly used to assess blood flow and investigate suspected clots in certain veins, including those in the legs.

### How long does an ultrasound scan take?

Many routine ultrasound examinations take approximately 15–30 minutes. More complex examinations may take longer.

### Do I need to fast before an ultrasound?

Some abdominal ultrasound examinations require fasting, while many other scans do not. Follow the instructions provided by your imaging facility.

### How much does an ultrasound scan cost in Uganda?

The cost varies by facility, scan type and location. Contact your preferred diagnostic imaging centre for its current price and preparation instructions.

## Conclusion

An ultrasound scan can provide valuable information about the structure and appearance of many organs, blood vessels and soft tissues. It is commonly used to investigate abdominal pain, assess pregnancy, examine pelvic organs, evaluate lumps and investigate blood flow abnormalities.

The type of ultrasound required depends on your symptoms and the clinical question your healthcare professional wants answered. If the findings are inconclusive, additional tests may be necessary.
